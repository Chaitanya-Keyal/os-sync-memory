CS F372 Assignment 3 - Problem 3 results
========================================

Setup
-----
VM: Ubuntu 24.04, kernel 6.14.0-forkguard, VirtualBox 1 vCPU, 2048 MiB RAM
(free reports 1976 MiB total, about 330 MiB of it used by the system when
idle). Page size 4096 B.
Swap: exactly one backing active per trial (checked by run_trials.sh),
4 GiB each. zswap disabled, no zram (swap_setup.sh does both).
  internal  /swapfile-a3, a 4 GiB swap file on the VM's root virtual disk,
            whose image lives on the host's internal NVMe SSD
  usb       a 4 GiB swap area on an external USB device (Part B)
Program: memswap, seed 42, 5 passes x 10,000 lookups. Every trial is a fresh
process; run_trials.sh syncs and drops the page cache and waits 5 s before
each one, and runs the trials one at a time.
Raw output of every trial: results/raw/<backing>_<size>_<pattern>.txt.
All phases of all trials: results/trials.csv.
All 5 checksums matched in every trial.

Sizes tested
  Part A, internal swap: 512, 1024, 1536, 2048, 2560 MiB, uniform and hot
  Part B, USB swap:      512, 1024, 1536 MiB, uniform and hot (PENDING)

Findings (Part A)
1. The transition lies between 1536 and 2048 MiB. About 1.6 GiB of RAM is
   free for the process. Up to 1536 MiB everything stays resident: no major
   faults, no swap traffic, lookups cost 15-150 ns (cache and TLB misses
   only; the first pass is slower because the TLB and caches are cold). At
   2048 MiB the initialization already writes 425 MiB to swap and every
   pass has thousands of major faults: about 120 µs per lookup for uniform,
   more than 1,000 times slower than at 1536 MiB.
2. Each major fault costs about 0.55-0.65 ms here (e.g. 2509 ms / 3858
   faults at 2560 MiB uniform): one 4 KiB read from the swap file through
   VirtualBox's virtual disk. Swap-in KiB is exactly 4 x the major faults in
   every pass, so every major fault was one page read from swap and swap
   readahead brought in nothing extra for these random patterns.
3. Uniform faults match the share of the allocation that is not in RAM:
   2163 of 10,000 lookups (22%) at 2048 MiB and 3858 (39%) at 2560 MiB,
   close to (size - ~1.6 GiB) / size = 22% and 38%. Uniform has no reuse,
   so pass 5 is no better than pass 1: each page swapped in pushes another
   one out, and the resident share stays the same.
4. The hot region is slower than uniform here, the opposite of what one
   might expect, and it improves from pass to pass. Initialization touches
   pages in order, so the hot region (the first 10% of pages) is the oldest
   memory and is the first to be swapped out: after initialization the hot
   region is entirely in swap. 9,000 of the 10,000 lookups go to it, but a
   pass touches only about 13% of its 65,536 pages (at 2560 MiB), so most
   hot lookups in pass 1 fault (8688 faults vs 3858 for uniform). Each pass
   pulls more of the hot region into RAM, and the faults fall: 8688, 7545,
   6737, 5895, 5113. A simple model predicts this closely: 9,000 random
   picks from 65,536 pages hit 8,400 distinct pages, a page is still
   unread after k passes with probability e^(-0.137k), and the 1,000 cold
   lookups add ~300 faults; that gives about 8710, 7650, 6720, 5915 and
   5210. Once the 256 MiB hot region were resident, which would take about
   20 passes, the hot pattern would see almost only the ~1,000 cold lookups
   fault and would beat uniform by a wide margin. Locality pays off only
   after the working set has been brought in.
5. Swap-out during the access passes happens almost only in pass 1 (93 MiB
   at 2560 uniform, 171 MiB at 2560 hot); later passes write little or
   nothing.
   Pages that were swapped in and not modified keep their copy in swap (the
   swap cache), so when the kernel evicts them again it can drop them
   without writing. The lookups only read.
6. Allocation takes only milliseconds: posix_memalign of 1 MiB uses mmap,
   which only reserves address space. Physical pages are allocated at first
   touch, which is why initialization holds the cost (0.3-1 s without
   pressure, 4.3-12.6 s with pressure because it also writes 410-912 MiB
   to swap).
7. Deallocation also has 230-360 major faults under pressure. free() reads
   glibc's chunk header, which sits just before each 1 MiB block in a page
   of its own. That page was written once at allocation time and never
   again, so it is a likely candidate to be swapped out, and reading it back
   is a major fault.
